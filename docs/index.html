EndoComparer: Comparative Surgical Video Question Answering with Pseudo-Siamese Visual Adaptation and Perceive-then-Compare Reasoning

TL;DR

EndoComparer is a vision-language model focused on cross-case surgical video comparison: given two surgical videos from different cases, it answers questions about whether and how they differ in anatomy, tools, actions and skill. EndoDiffVQA is the benchmark we built for this task.

Abstract

Purpose: Surgical training benefits from explicit comparison between cases: in patient condition, in technique and in skill proficiency. Surgical vision-language models (VLMs) offer a promising and flexible interface for this purpose. Yet current VLMs typically see one video at a time, and they often fall short when asked to compare videos.

Methods: We propose EndoComparer, a VLM for comparative surgical video question answering with two components. (a) A Pseudo-Siamese Visual LoRA (PSVL) gives each video slot its own low-rank adapter over a shared frozen vision encoder, separating the two videos in the visual pathway at the cost of a single encoder pass. (b) A Perceive-then-Compare chain of thought (PtC-CoT) trains the model to first state what each video shows, then derive the answer from these statements. We further curate EndoDiffVQA, a benchmark of nearly 11K multiple-choice questions on video pairs over cholecystectomy and gastric bypass procedures, covering anatomy, tool, action and skill.

Results: EndoComparer outperforms strong generalist and surgical VLMs on detecting and specifying differences between videos. Ablations show that the two proposed components contribute complementarily, and the model remains more robust under a video-swap control that exposes confusion between the two videos.

Conclusion: Providing a dedicated visual reasoning path through PSVL and PtC-CoT makes cross-case comparison of surgical videos more robust for VLMs. EndoDiffVQA and EndoComparer provide a benchmark and a strong baseline for this task.
